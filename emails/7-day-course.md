## DAY 4
**Subject: The 3pm crash is not about willpower. It's about your 12pm lunch.**

Hi {{first_name}},

Sleepy at 3. Craving something sweet at 4. Wondering what's wrong with your discipline by 5.

Nothing is wrong with your discipline. Here's the chain: a carb-heavy lunch spikes your glucose, your body over-corrects with insulin, and the over-correction drops you BELOW where you started. That dip is the crash. The craving is your body demanding fast fuel to climb out. It's chemistry, running exactly as designed, on the fuel schedule it was given.

Two free levers, starting tomorrow:

Eat lunch in order: vegetables or salad first, protein second, carbs last. Same food, gentler curve. Studies on meal sequencing show post-meal glucose reductions of 30 to 40% from order alone.

Then walk 10 minutes after. Your muscles pull glucose out of the blood during movement, no insulin required.

## DAY 5
**Subject: One week of bad sleep can make your labs look prediabetic**

Hi {{first_name}},

If I could only fix one habit for most of the people I teach, it would not be food.

It would be sleep.

In research settings, restricting healthy adults to 4 to 5 hours a night measurably reduced insulin sensitivity within a single week. Their glucose handling drifted toward the impaired range. Nothing about their diet changed.

Short sleep also turns up next-day hunger hormones and cravings for exactly the foods that spike you. So the "bad food day" that follows a bad night was rigged before breakfast.

The fix starts at the wrong end of the night: anchor your WAKE time, seven days a week, even weekends. Bedtime follows a stable wake time. Then close the kitchen 2 to 3 hours before bed.
