# The Functional Medicine Approach to Blood Pressure
## Target: "functional medicine blood pressure" | DRAFT pending Dr. Hunter's clinical review, then blog-publisher pipeline.

**TL;DR:** A functional medicine approach to blood pressure asks WHY the pressure is elevated rather than only measuring THAT it is: it maps the lifestyle inputs (sodium load, sleep debt, stress chemistry, sedentary time, insulin resistance) that raise vascular workload, and addresses those roots through education while your own physician manages diagnosis and treatment. It complements medical care; it never replaces it.

*By Dr. Shallanda Hunter, PharmD, MBA, CFNMP | Last updated July 2026*

## What does "functional medicine approach" actually mean for blood pressure?

It means asking why. Conventional care measures your blood pressure, categorizes it against the AHA/ACC thresholds, and treats the number, often skillfully and often necessarily. The functional approach adds the question the 10-minute visit rarely has time for: what in this person's daily patterns is raising vascular workload, and which of those inputs can education change?

Both approaches matter. This is not either/or, and anyone who frames it that way is selling something. Your physician manages the diagnosis and any medication. Education works the roots underneath, and the two together outperform either alone.

## The five roots a functional lens examines

**1. Sodium and the modern food supply.** Most dietary sodium comes from packaged and restaurant food, not the salt shaker. Consistently high intake raises blood volume and vascular pressure in salt-sensitive people. The educational lever: whole-food swaps and label literacy, aiming under 2,300 mg per day.

**2. Sleep debt.** Blood pressure normally dips overnight; short and fragmented sleep blunts that dip and raises daytime readings. Sleep is not a wellness accessory here; it is a direct hemodynamic input.

**3. Stress chemistry.** Cortisol and adrenaline raise pressure by design: fuel and pressure for a threat response. A life of unburned threat responses is a life of elevated readings. Ten scheduled minutes of daily decompression is not soft advice; it targets the mechanism.

**4. Sedentary architecture.** Movement conditions the vessels themselves. Regular moderate activity (150 or more minutes weekly, walkable in 10-minute pieces) is one of the most consistent non-drug influences on blood pressure in the research.

**5. Insulin resistance, the quiet partner.** Elevated insulin promotes sodium retention and vascular changes, which is why blood pressure and blood sugar so often creep together. Working the metabolic root (meal composition and order, fiber, post-meal movement) frequently pays rent in BOTH numbers.

## What this looks like in practice

Measure consistently: same time each morning, seated, logged, because a trend teaches what a single reading cannot. Then work one root at a time for two weeks each rather than overhauling life in a weekend. ## The hard line, stated plainly

Nothing here replaces medical care. Readings at or above 180/120 warrant prompt medical attention. Never start, stop, or change blood pressure medication except with your prescriber; some medications must never be stopped abruptly. The functional lens makes you an educated participant in your care, and educated participants have better conversations, not fewer doctors.

## FAQ

**Can blood pressure be lowered without medication?**
Lifestyle inputs measurably influence blood pressure, especially in the elevated and stage 1 ranges, and your physician decides whether medication is also needed. Many people do both; the roots work regardless.

**Which matters more: salt or stress?**
It varies by person, which is the entire point of the functional lens: your log reveals YOUR loudest input. Salt sensitivity, sleep debt, and stress load differ between people.

**How fast do lifestyle changes show up in readings?**
Some inputs (sleep, a hard sodium reduction) can shift readings within days to weeks; others build over months. The trend line, not any single morning, is the honest scorekeeper.

**Is "borderline" blood pressure worth acting on?**
The elevated and stage 1 zones are exactly where lifestyle influence is strongest in the research. Borderline is not a verdict; it is the window.
