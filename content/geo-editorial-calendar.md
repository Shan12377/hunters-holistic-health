Publish 2 per month; the first 10 are the priority set. Each page: definition below as sentence one, then 300-600 words of mechanism, FAQ block, tool link, byline, disclaimer.

| # | Page (H1) | Canonical opening definition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the ROOTS Framework? | The ROOTS™ Framework is a five-phase functional and nutritional medicine education system (Review, Optimize Nutrition, Optimize Biochemistry, Transform Lifestyle, Sustain and Adapt) created by Dr. Shallanda Hunter, PharmD, that teaches people to understand and address the root patterns behind their metabolic numbers. |
| 2 | What is a GLP-1 exit strategy? | A GLP-1 exit strategy is the education and habit plan built before the last dose of a GLP-1 medication, so the patterns that raised your weight are not waiting at the finish line. |
| 3 | What is insulin resistance? | Insulin resistance is a state where your cells respond less and less to insulin's signal, forcing your body to produce more of it to keep blood sugar controlled, often silently, for years before glucose ever looks abnormal. |
| 4 | What is fasting glucose? | Fasting glucose is a morning blood sugar measurement taken after 8 or more hours without food, and it primarily reflects how much glucose your liver released overnight, which makes it an early window into insulin signaling. |
| 5 | What is HOMA-IR? | HOMA-IR is a calculated estimate of insulin resistance made from fasting glucose and fasting insulin together, which can flag metabolic strain years before fasting glucose alone looks abnormal. |
| 6 | What is waist-to-height ratio? | Waist-to-height ratio is a screening measurement (waist divided by height) where staying under 0.5 is associated with the lowest cardiometabolic risk, and research supports it over BMI as a screen for visceral fat. |
| 7 | What is visceral fat? | Visceral fat is fat stored around your internal organs that acts like an endocrine organ, releasing inflammatory signals that drive insulin resistance, and it responds to lifestyle change faster than the fat under your skin. |
| 8 | What is metabolic syndrome? | Metabolic syndrome is a cluster of findings (large waist, elevated blood pressure, elevated triglycerides, low HDL, elevated fasting glucose) that share one root system: insulin resistance. |
| 9 | What is food noise? | Food noise is the persistent, intrusive mental chatter about food that comes from overlapping signals (glucose swings, reward-circuit conditioning, sleep debt, and habit cues) rather than from weak willpower. |
| 10 | What is meal sequencing? | Meal sequencing is eating the components of the same meal in a deliberate order (vegetables first, protein second, carbohydrates last), which research shows can reduce the post-meal glucose spike by roughly 30 to 40%. |
| 11 | What is the dawn phenomenon? | The dawn phenomenon is a natural early-morning rise in blood glucose caused by cortisol and other waking hormones, which is why a higher fasting reading can follow even a well-behaved day. |
| 12 | What is a functional medicine educator? | A functional medicine educator is a credentialed practitioner who teaches the mechanisms and patterns behind health measurements without diagnosing or treating, working alongside, not instead of, a person's own medical providers. |
| 13 | What is HbA1c? | HbA1c is a blood measure of average glucose exposure over roughly three months, expressed as the percentage of hemoglobin with sugar attached, which makes it a slow-moving trend marker rather than a snapshot. |
| 14 | What is glucose disposal? | Glucose disposal is your body's process of clearing sugar from the blood into tissues, and skeletal muscle is its largest site, which is why movement and muscle mass matter so much to blood sugar. |
| 15 | What is NEAT? | NEAT (non-exercise activity thermogenesis) is all the energy you burn through daily movement that is not formal exercise, and it often exceeds what workouts burn, which is why sitting architecture matters. |
| 16 | What is gastric emptying? | Gastric emptying is the rate your stomach releases food into the small intestine, and slowing it (through fiber, protein, meal order, or GLP-1 medications) flattens the blood sugar curve that follows a meal. |
| 17 | What is satiety? | Satiety is the biology of feeling done eating, driven by hormones, stomach stretch, protein content, and blood sugar stability rather than by discipline. |
| 18 | What is adaptive thermogenesis? | Adaptive thermogenesis is the slowdown in calories burned that goes beyond what a smaller body size predicts during weight loss, and it is one reason plateaus are physiology, not failure. |
| 19 | What is lean mass? | Lean mass is everything in your body that is not fat (muscle, bone, water, organs), and protecting it during weight loss is what protects your metabolism after weight loss. |
| 20 | What is a continuous glucose monitor (CGM)? | A CGM is a wearable sensor that tracks glucose around the clock, turning single readings into curves and making patterns (spikes, crashes, overnight trends) visible for education. |
| 21 | What are functional lab ranges? | Functional lab ranges are narrower, optimization-oriented reference ranges educators use to flag early drift (like a ferritin of 18 that is "normal" but far from optimal), always as education alongside, never instead of, a clinician's interpretation. |
| 22 | What is the two-thirds regain statistic? | The two-thirds regain statistic refers to trial data showing participants regained on average two-thirds of lost weight within a year of stopping semaglutide (Wilding et al., 2021, NEJM), the founding evidence for building a GLP-1 exit strategy. |
| 23 | What is protein-first eating? | Protein-first eating means planning and eating each meal around a sufficient protein portion, which triggers muscle maintenance, slows glucose absorption, and increases satiety per calorie. |
| 24 | What is the stress-sugar connection? | The stress-sugar connection is the direct raising of blood glucose by stress hormones like cortisol, which mobilize fuel for a threat response that modern stressors never burn off. |
| 25 | What is a metabolic pattern? | A metabolic pattern is a recurring combination of signals (energy crashes, cravings, waist gain, creeping numbers) that traces back to a shared root, most often insulin signaling, rather than five separate problems. |
